## Hook (0:00)

**[015]** What if the smartest guy in the room woke up one day as a dragon? And not a metaphorical one. Wings, scales, the whole thing.

**[013]** That's exactly what happens to our main character, and honestly, his reaction is the best part. Let's get into it.

## The limit of human knowledge

**[003]** Meet our guy, a researcher passed out at his desk, buried in notes. The comic opens with him asking what he calls the single most powerful question a being can ask, the kind of question that opens up whole new realms of knowledge.

**[004]** The problem? The more you ask it, the harder the answers get. He's reading theories about tearing space to enter the fourth dimension, and his honest reaction is basically: "I'd believe this if aliens wrote it."

**[005]** He admits that no matter how well he understood the math, there was always something he couldn't wrap his head around. That was the limit of human understanding.

**[006]** And then the comic hits us with this metaphor: human knowledge is like a well slowly drying up.

**[007]** Not because the water ran out, but because nobody ever refilled it. People never fed their curiosity.

**[008]** Instead, they were just fed what came before them and told to add to the pile. Which, if you've ever written a research paper, yeah, that one hurts.

## The day everything changed

**[009]** Then we get a glimpse of something very wrong: a lab in ruins, cables torn down, equipment wrecked, and that same machine sitting in the middle of it all.

**[011]** And he drops the line that sets up the whole series: the day he met his end, in an experiment meant to change humanity's future, his understanding of the world shattered.

**[012]** And his first thought is a very reasonable "...I'm not dead?"

**[013]** Followed by a much less reasonable one: "I'm... a dragon?"

**[015]** Everything he'd ever studied turned out to be just one piece of the puzzle. Because he's now in a world of magic, where creatures the size of skyscrapers rule the skies...

**[017]** ...and trees grow as big as cities. A world where physics is only a small part of the story.

## Rewind: how he got here

**[020]** Then the comic rewinds. Alarm clock beeping, our guy slaps it off, half asleep, and mumbles: "Is today the day?"

**[021]** We learn he's been working nonstop on a program in something called dimensional science, at DARPA, of all places, where some of the greatest minds in the world work together.

**[022]** And even there, this project was controversial. He usually keeps up with the other scientists, but this one? He openly says it makes no sense. But he's in too deep to back out now. His badge tells us his name: Jack Notorea.

**[023]** So he does what every tired employee does. Shows up, and decides: "All I have to do is follow instructions." Spoiler: that's not going to work out.

**[025]** Thirty-six hours later, we see the setup: a single object packed with every sensor a physicist could imagine.

**[026]** It's going to be the test subject of the world's most secretive experiment ever conducted.

**[027]** Jack asks how long until it's ready...

**[028]** ...and the answer is five hours and fifty minutes until the capacitors are fully charged. And if you remember how this chapter started, you already know this countdown isn't ending well for him.

## Outro

**[013]** So, does the experiment explain how a DARPA scientist ends up hatching as a dragon? We'll find out in the next part. Let me know your theories, and go read The Dragon of Dreams to support the creator!
